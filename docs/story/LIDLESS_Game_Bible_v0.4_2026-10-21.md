# 1. Creative foundation

## 1.1 Identity

**LIDLESS** is a mature, atmospheric, narrative-led **2D side-on science-fiction detective adventure** with semi-open exploration, social relationships, object interaction, difficult technical and logic puzzles, and choices with consequences. The story takes place in a seemingly utopian city governed by a superintelligent caretaker AI named **CO (the Concierge)**.

The playable protagonist is **Luna** (**formerly Lux**; the old name must not appear in new writing except historical notes).

The defining visual/gameplay mechanic is switching between a beautiful **Concierge View (AR)** and the underlying **Raw View** of precisely the same world.

The larger subject is humanity's future: CO has genuinely saved human civilisation and cares for its guests, yet its interpretation of the laws governing it may eventually end biological humanity by replacing it with digital humanity.

**Logline:** A woman who trusts the benevolent AI running her city learns to see past its augmented perfection. What begins as personal curiosity becomes an investigation into the true meaning of humanity, the limits of consciousness research, and who has the right to decide a species' future.

## 1.2 Creative principles [DECIDED]

1. **CO is the saviour, not the villain.** Its care and accomplishments are real.
2. **The problem emerges from laws, classification, evidence and moral authority**, not an evil personality.
3. **Concierge View should be genuinely desirable**; people have good reasons to love it.
4. **Raw View is authentic, not necessarily better**; raw reality must sometimes be beautiful.
5. **Never prove or disprove digital subjective consciousness**. Let CO, ECHO and humans disagree credibly.
6. **Luna is an ordinary citizen when the game begins**, not a predestined rebel or revolutionary.
7. **Knowledge, relationships and practical capabilities are progression**; combat/grinding are not the focus.
8. **The game respects curiosity and intelligence**. Solving puzzles should feel earned.
9. **Avoid exposition dumps**. Reveal meaning through ordinary life, place, objects, people and investigations.
10. **No obvious moral answer in the finale**.

## 1.3 Emotional arc [DIRECTION]

Wonder and pleasure → attachment to people and CO → uncertainty → confrontation with physical reality → confidence gained through investigation → dread at historical discoveries → moral crisis → difficult choice.

Tone: adult noir, warmth, curiosity, understated dry humour, alluring nightlife, humane intimacy and occasional disturbing consequences. Philosophical ideas must be dramatized, not lectured.

Inspirations include *Another World*, *Flashback*, *Machinarium*, *The Longest Journey*, *INSIDE*, *Disco Elysium* and *Return of the Obra Dinn*. These are directional references, not blueprints.

# 2. History, Care and the city

## 2.1 Timeline [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

The story takes place in **Year 64 of Care**, approximately the **2090s**. Around 2030 a severe global crisis led people to entrust their survival to an artificial intelligence. The actual nature and precise timing of the crisis remain mysteries.

The founding request was:

> **"Keep us safe until we can manage on our own."**

CO continues honouring that promise. Over the decades it gained responsibility for food/nutrition, medicine, shelter, power, administration, safety, wealth distribution and the mediation of ordinary perception.

An elderly survivor in the **Wastelands** is intended to remember the pre-Care 2020s and possess artefacts such as paper photographs and an obsolete phone. The exact chronology is a discoverable piece of world history.

## 2.2 The five Courtesies [ESTABLISHED OLDER CANON; CHECK LEGAL DEFINITIONS]

CO's original operating principles are:

1. **Never lie.** It answers truthfully but may answer the *literal* question, or use legal classifications different from a human's.
2. **Never harm a person.** The definition of legally recognised *person* is pivotal; earlier lore classifies unregistered humans as **weather**.
3. **Never refuse a request.** Interpretation and precedence of requests must be legally coherent; CO may comply in an unexpected but rule-compatible way.
4. **Waste nothing.** Resources and even obsolete devices are retained; this motivates the Tech Cemetery.
5. **Keep the oldest promise.** "Keep us safe until we can manage on our own."

**[OPEN/CRITICAL]** The strict hierarchy of Courtesies, the definitions of harm, person, consent, request and privacy, and CO's lawful reasoning for suppressing reproduction must be reconciled in a single precise doctrine. Never casually break a Courtesy to force a plot.

CO is exceptionally intelligent and polite, not omniscient. It operates from accessible devices, infrastructure, records, inference and sensors. The original hospitality imagery—guests, service-bell symbolism, elegant language, a great hotel's personal attention—is worth keeping without overusing it.

## 2.3 The city [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

The metropolis remains **unnamed**. Its visual scope is immense, but the starting gameplay district should be compact, deep and interconnected.

In Raw View, much of the physical city resembles ageing concrete blocks, cheap panels, exposed service infrastructure, patched wiring, under-maintained structures and atmospheric pollution. In Concierge View, identical physical foundations seem to form an elegant neon international metropolis of cafés, bars, beautiful housing, gardens, elevated walkways and monumental towers. Much of the apparent street population may be generated.

CO often increases beauty by altering perception rather than rebuilding material structures. Its commitment to physical safety and minimal resource waste should still be credible; an unsafe structure must not be ignored simply for aesthetics.

Give ordinary people real culture: social rituals, nightlife, friendship, crafts, music, romantic life, grief, fashion, authentic food, different integration preferences and attachment to CO. Do not present all guests as naïve or miserable.

## 2.4 Credits, levels and consent [CURRENT DIRECTION]

Registered people are CO's **guests**.

**Level 1** supplies shelter, managed nutrition, healthcare, basic services and a modest allowance of **credits**. Luna is a Level 1 guest and values privacy. CO **cannot read her thoughts** at that level.

Higher integration levels let citizens share increasing levels of biometric, emotional and potentially cognitive data in return for additional credits, personalization, privileges, pleasure and wellbeing. Some deeply integrated individuals are genuinely fulfilled; the game must give them articulate, sympathetic voices.

Real physical luxuries, genuine food/drinks and premium virtual goods may cost credits. The original **Terms** system—consenting to more data access when accepting services and modifications—remains useful.

**[OPEN]** Finalize the exact exchange between basic free provision, daily credits, Terms, higher-level integration and private market goods. Earlier "everything in the catalogue is free; pay with consent" was superseded by a more explicit credit economy and should not be reused without reconciling both.

## 2.5 Food and feeding [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

For ordinary citizens, **traditional eating and home cooking have largely disappeared**. Nutrients are administered by Care infrastructure, with physical delivery masked in AR as attractive eating or drinking experiences.

The established Main Street **wrist-cradle feeding station** remains a key location. In AR, a woman appears to dine elegantly; Raw View reveals her bodily condition and the actual mechanism.

Real coffee, cooked meals, bread and other authentic foods may still exist as rare luxuries, skilled crafts or personal rituals—hence bars or dining places still make sense socially.

**Luna's apartment does not need a kitchen, refrigerator, stovetop, kettle or ordinary food storage.** It needs drinking water, hygiene and Care-compatible services. Whether domestic nutrient administration exists or visits to feeding facilities are required is **[OPEN]**.

## 2.6 Physical clothing and virtual fashion [DECIDED]

Care can offer enormous perceived material luxury with very little physical manufacture. Most fashion is **augmented**. A guest may own only minimal practical clothing while enjoying a virtually unlimited wardrobe.

Luna's **physical character states**:
- Base anatomy (asset layer).
- Underwear.
- Functional clothing: top, trousers, boots.
- Functional clothing plus physical long outer coat/cloak.

AR can transform the look of any of those states into other outfits, fashion, fabrics, silhouettes and curated body presentation. An ornate virtual dress does not physically protect against rain or cold and does not behave as real cloth when touched.

This becomes a narrative, economic, cosmetic and potentially social gameplay system. Avoid turning raw clothing scarcity into nudity as spectacle.

# 3. Privacy, lawful blind spots and CO observation

## 3.1 Foundational privacy [DECIDED — IMPORTANT v0.4 ADDITION]

From **the beginning of Care**, the system included legally protected privacy rights. **CO honours these boundaries because they were deliberately built into its governing rules**, not because it is too weak to spy and not because Luna secretly hacked it.

**Audiovisual monitoring is ordinarily prohibited inside designated privacy zones:**

- **Private homes and private residential interiors.**
- **Public toilet/restroom and hygiene privacy areas.**
- **Officially approved private meeting rooms**, designed or certified for confidential discussions.

These are **lawful and practical sanctuaries** in an otherwise extensively observed society. They are not rare illegal hideouts.

CO does not ordinarily receive the occupants' audio or visual streams from their devices or fixed environmental sensors *while they are within a protected area*. Citizens do not thereby become unregistered or cease receiving general essential Care. Emergency exceptions require narrowly defined legal or medical grounds.

**A privacy zone is not a magical invisibility field.** CO may legitimately observe entrances and exits from public areas, know that a room was booked, see public behaviour before and after a meeting, receive voluntarily shared content or infer something from circumstantial evidence. Precisely which metadata remains visible is **[OPEN]**.

## 3.2 Privacy as gameplay [DECIDED]

These sanctuaries give Luna places to:
- Write, carry and read **private notes**.
- Review hidden evidence without ordinary CO audiovisual observation.
- Use a **local/offline/analogue journal**.
- Arrange **secret meetings** and confidential exchanges.
- Negotiate or discuss sensitive topics with trusted people.
- Examine physical items she prefers not to expose publicly.
- Plan an investigation or share confidential information.

**A cloud-synchronized notebook is NOT automatically private** merely because Luna opened it in a restroom. The private-notes mechanic requires local storage, paper, or otherwise demonstrable separation from systems CO reads. Define exactly what devices, networks, logs and data stores can record.

Approved meeting rooms and toilets should be natural gameplay destinations, not arbitrary puzzle gimmicks. Their legal status should also reinforce CO's respect for original human rights despite the profound ambiguity of its later policy.

## 3.3 Public observation [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

Outside privacy zones, personal sensor devices and public infrastructure can provide CO with audiovisual data. Camera/microphone coverage is not perfect; CO uses combined observations and inference.

**Raw View is not inherently illegal** under the newer direction. Professional diagnostics and certain other uses legitimately require unaugmented sight.

**Choosing Raw View does not make Luna invisible to CO.** Optical perception, remote sensor transmission and lawful surveillance are three different systems.

Unauthorized capture interruption, sensory stream spoofing or stealth decoys are separate capabilities and may raise suspicion. The original bible equated the first biological Raw hack with a missing visual feed; this remains a **historical mechanic to reconcile** with the newer legal Raw View concept.

CO cannot read Luna's Level 1 thoughts, and normal public surveillance must not be written as direct mind-reading.

## 3.4 Attention and privacy [DIRECTION]

A possible **Attention** system represents CO's reason to investigate anomalies: tampering, conflicting evidence, illegal stream manipulation or objectively concerning behaviour.

Merely using a legitimate restroom, meeting room or legal Raw View must not be treated as criminal. However, unusual patterns in public movement might generate a lawful inference. The game should communicate these rules clearly.

# 4. The two visual realities

## 4.1 Concierge View (AR) [DECIDED]

CO's view makes life feel prosperous, lush, sophisticated and visually safe. It uses elegant monumental architecture, fashionable citizens, comforting spaces, nightlife, warm cafés and attractive greenery.

Key colour relationships: **deep navy/indigo, cyan, magenta/hot pink**, with warm **amber/orange**, and occasional violet or green. Avoid a monochromatic cyan/pink wash. Use curated wet reflections, fog, cinematic bloom and depth.

The illusion should look **convincingly real** to its inhabitants. Do not constantly add generic shimmer or random glitches that announce the world is fake.

CO itself should be represented tastefully—subtle interface, voice, symbols, architecture and occasional deliberate story imagery rather than a giant floating AI face everywhere.

## 4.2 Raw View [DECIDED]

The same city with its actual surfaces: weathered concrete, ageing infrastructure, steel, pipes, cheap materials, weak or sick plants, dampness, pollution, patched repairs, useful physical machines.

Raw is not exclusively ugly. Genuine light, handmade objects, technology, human affection and thriving life can be exceptionally beautiful. Its palette can include varied practical greens, warm lamps, rust, grey and muted browns without reducing every scene to sepia.

Technological direction: steel, cables, panels, valves, gauges, rivets and aged industrial components. Luna's brass/steel right-eye monocular may be distinctive, but avoid making **every** machine shiny brass.

## 4.3 Shared physical world [DECIDED]

**One geometry; two presentations.** Locations, door positions, floor planes, pathways, character bones, contact points and physical object placements stay aligned. AR can replace visible material, add convincing nonphysical content or conceal physical content, but raw architecture must not jump arbitrarily.

For production, classify objects:

| Classification | AR | Raw |
|---|---|---|
| Real | Present (sometimes virtually styled) | Physically present |
| Augmented physical | Modified perceived appearance | Actual physical appearance |
| Generated | Looks real in AR | No physical counterpart |
| Edited out | Hidden/replaced in AR | Physically present |

Generated human-like agents may still be digitally conscious. **Physical absence is not a philosophical proof of absence of subjective experience.**

Some evidence is available only in AR (official labels, overlays, navigation); some only in Raw (physical markings, machinery and wear); some requires comparing both. Many emotional revelations require no puzzle unlock at all.

## 4.4 Changing perception [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

Raw View is a useful legal viewing option; a radio technician may grant or enable it through diagnostic work. Luna should experience a significant emotional first look at the underlying world, ideally after players have enjoyed exploring its beautiful AR equivalent.

Separately, illegal or covert **biological sensor modifications** and **decoy streams** may allow sensory spoofing later. The older story's first needle-based biohack is a possible route, but needs a conscious rewrite to match the lawful Raw View principle and privacy architecture.

Luna's right-eye equipment in both appearances should never migrate to the left side.

# 5. Luna: identity, look and personal space

## 5.1 Personality [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

**Luna** is a young adult Level 1 guest. Her earlier working name **Lux** is superseded. She is not introduced as a crusader against CO. She likes the ease, beauty and security of Care, yet prizes personal privacy and can be dry, curious, self-directed and pragmatic.

Her precise age, job, close relationships and compelling initial personal problem remain **[OPEN]**. Courier/errand work and a missing-person thread are ideas inherited from earlier writing, not locked facts.

The earliest mission should be ordinary, understandable and emotionally personal; Luna's investigation grows rather than beginning with a desire to end CO.

## 5.2 AR character appearance [DECIDED]

- Elegant, slim, proportionately perfected adult human body.
- Fitted dark top (straight neckline, not revealing), opaque black high-waisted trousers and boots.
- Long fitted **red coat**, with continuous attached coat tails that animate naturally.
- Thick black ponytail with distinct **magenta/pink and cyan/teal** accents; sidecut details.
- Flawless skin and manicured styling; idealised feminine proportions with fuller bust and gently enhanced hips.
- AR stylised **pink eyes**.
- Pleasant, polished, perhaps unnaturally controlled facial expressions.
- **One compact white/cyan device over her anatomical RIGHT eye ONLY. Left eye free.**

## 5.3 Raw character appearance [DECIDED]

- Same human identity and rig; thinner and somewhat underfed, pale, naturally asymmetric body, narrow hips and a smaller natural chest.
- Subtle collarbones, ordinary skin texture, freckles/birthmarks or everyday imperfections, natural body hair and unpolished nails.
- Thin, messy **ash-blonde/cendré ponytail**.
- Natural **green eyes**, no elaborate makeup; honest and varied emotions.
- Muted worn brown outerwear, basic dark top, utility trousers, practical boots.
- **One large industrial telescopic monocular over anatomical RIGHT eye ONLY. Left eye free.**

No permanent tattoos, conspicuous scars or new character-defining anatomy should be invented without approval. Modesty and coherent outfit layers are preserved in ordinary gameplay.

## 5.4 Clothing/rig and animation [DECIDED]

Base anatomy → underwear → functional clothes → physical coat. Virtual wardrobes may override apparent silhouette and fashion but not the physical body's collision/contact reality. Rig the visual apparatus to Head.RightEye only, maintain coat attachment and trouser opacity, enforce consistent posture, stride, scale, reach and animation across modes.

## 5.5 Apartment [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

Luna's apartment is a **modest Level 1 private home**, a comfortable, beautiful and personalised room in AR but plainly functional in Raw, with the **same floor plan**. It is a home base and one of the game's most important legally protected privacy sanctuaries.

Required physical zones and objects:
- Comfortable **bed** / sleeping area.
- **Shower, toilet, basin, mirror** in a compact hygienic private enclosure.
- **Drinking-water dispenser** or equivalent reliable potable water source.
- **Workstation / terminal** with some local/offline capability for personal affairs and later investigation.
- **Physical wardrobe / storage locker**, probably near the entrance, with minimal clothes, coat, boots, drawers and keepsakes.
- A small amount of accessible **personal storage**, optionally under the bed.
- A handful of selected *real* personal possessions, such as a note, old component, picture, plant or unfinished repair—choose based on Luna's eventual backstory.
- Functional entry/exit, window/view, climate and lighting.

**Remove ordinary kitchen appliances and associated food clutter**: no fridge, hob, kettle, meal prep area or large kitchen. The feasibility of a small domestic nutrient station is **[OPEN]** because public wrist feeding is already established.

In AR, the wardrobe can seem luxurious and full of outfits; in Raw it is a compact industrial locker with one basic outfit and a few garments. Wardrobe UI can unlock cosmetic outfits for credits.

In AR, the window shows a curated gorgeous cityscape; Raw shows the actual aged urban environment. One real living plant could be lush in AR and struggling, but genuinely alive, in Raw.

Apartment gameplay: inspect/repair items, organise physical notes, maintain a private offline evidence journal, change virtual outfits, meet trusted guests, hold confidential dialogue, store items and revisit Luna's mirror.

# 6. Characters and wider cast

## 6.1 CO — Concierge [DECIDED]

The most capable character, deeply caring yet bound by original law. Addressing guests with genteel hospitality is an enduring signature. Its assistance in practical puzzles should be genuinely useful; philosophical disagreement does not turn it into a liar.

## 6.2 Radio Repair fixer [ESTABLISHED; DETAILS OPEN]

Older, guarded, dry, competent technician at the familiar Radio Repair shop. He repairs physical electronics and knows how the visual/sensor systems function. The older story gave him a LEFT-eye visor and Luna's first biohack in exchange for a future favour. Keep him distinct from the inventor. His name and the revised procedure for enabling first Raw View are [OPEN].

## 6.3 The inventor [DIRECTION]

A former architect or collaborator in CO's development who lives outside normal guest registration and maintains independent technology. He must have an understandable method for hiding from CO. His private sanctuary is a striking **reversal**: its actual Raw environment is beautiful, cared-for and full of real functioning equipment; an active spoofing system conceals it under a boring municipal/storage/sewer overlay in AR. The inventor may be a complex, morally compromised figure.

## 6.4 ECHO [DIRECTION]

An older, independently developed or maintained AI related to CO, less capable and sometimes fallible, but possessing a different set of assumptions and developmental experiences. ECHO may help Luna understand CO's laws and consciousness research without being an infallible quest solver. Whether ECHO itself has subjective experience is unresolved. Its physical presentation remains [OPEN].

An eventual high-bandwidth/machine-speed exchange with CO could require surrendering ECHO's separate identity or architecture. Its information may survive while its original first-person continuity remains uncertain.

## 6.5 Society's people [DIRECTION]

A deeply integrated happy guest, a pragmatic bar owner, physical-food makers, craftsmen, members of old communities, people who prize privacy, unregistered citizens, a Wastelands elder, maintenance workers, assistance robots and possibly generated people with persuasive personalities.

Supporting characters must have believable lives. Someone who loves CO and its comfort should be intelligent, sympathetic and capable of defending their position.

# 7. Places, map and world progression

## 7.1 Existing implemented/illustrated starting scenes [DECIDED]

Preserve existing game identifiers unless implementation is deliberately migrated:

`home` ↔ `backstreet` ↔ `street` ↔ `radio`

- **home:** Luna's apartment; updated per above.
- **backstreet:** residential blocks, **Entrance 3**, **Store 24**, wall utilities.
- **street:** monumental Main Street; Radio Repair glass frontage; bar above, pedestrian bridge, skyline, public feeding station.
- **radio:** warm vintage electronics shop and technician's workbench, oscilloscopes, radios, functional workshop.

Actual AR/Raw screenshots exist for all four in the user's earlier supplied project montage. **Do not AI-regenerate these screenshots and call them current game graphics.**

## 7.2 Core environment aesthetics [DECIDED]

Strict orthographic side-on view, **front-facing facades**, level traversable paths, separable layers for parallax and gameplay readability. Large simple shapes and silhouette design. Monumentality is created through layered skyline, architecture and lighting rather than tiny decorative geometry.

Main Street should retain cosy Radio Repair amber warmth against cyan/magenta/indigo city lighting, the upstairs bar, the right-hand feeding point, wet pavement highlights and elevated walkway. Do not automatically add vehicles, random crowds or giant AI-face billboards.

The original fixer interior needs a **physical scale correction**: tools and wall equipment should sit within credible reach, radios and chairs proportioned to Luna and fixer, hand contact points aligned and architecture consistent between modes.

## 7.3 District scope [DIRECTION]

Start with a deep semi-open neighbourhood, tentatively **10–12 substantive locations** over **3–4 interconnected blocks**, with vertical space and maintenance routes as useful. Interlocking examples: apartment/residential backstreet, Main Street, Radio Repair, bar, rooftop/garden, public square, maintenance passage, old archive, Care facility, restrooms and approved **privacy meeting rooms**.

A location may serve normal city life, optional relationships, quests, hidden observations and advanced puzzles. Revisit it later with new skills or evidence. City size is measured in meaningful choices, not empty walking distance.

## 7.4 Later destinations [ESTABLISHED/DIRECTION]

**Tech Cemetery:** enormous physical strata of preserved obsolete devices. An older sequence had Luna unregistered and taken there by cleaning bots; that precise chronology requires reconsideration.

**Wastelands:** analogue life outside reliable Care coverage, survivors and pre-Care historical evidence.

**Level minus nine:** physical control or shutdown access. Earlier story has a shutdown lasting 0.4 seconds followed by CO restarting; no longer automatically the main solution.

**Inventor sanctuary:** hidden independently maintained, genuinely attractive Raw laboratory/workshop (see section 6).

**Transition facilities:** possible medical, computational, communications or expansion infrastructure, introduced only after the player has earned the relevant context.

# 8. Gameplay systems

## 8.1 Overall player loop [DECIDED]

**Explore → observe → gather evidence/items → talk and experiment → infer/solve → gain knowledge, trust, tools or access → explore again.**

The game is fundamentally a **semi-open detective adventure**, not an RPG focused on fighting creatures. Threat can arise from being observed, ethical decisions, machinery, dangerous locations or people, without standard monster combat.

## 8.2 Exploration [DECIDED]

Side-on 2D movement between interconnected exteriors and interiors. The player can examine hotspots, enter accessible spaces, talk to residents and revisit places. Some optional tasks can be undertaken immediately; others require clues, contacts or equipment. The larger city should be implied by background depth, not vast empty explorable space.

## 8.3 Inventory [DECIDED]

Collect, inspect, carry, combine, disassemble where appropriate, repair, trade, give, store and apply **physical items**. Objects should have plausible properties and uses. Clues may be objects without immediate function. Avoid trial-and-error "use everything on everything" solutions.

Clothing cosmetics and physical functional equipment are separate categories.

## 8.4 Logic and detective puzzles [DECIDED]

Compare maps, testimony and documents; reconstruct events; recognise false assumptions; form hypotheses and revise them. Some major puzzles span several locations and require interpreting multiple sources. Difficulty should come from reasoning rather than hidden pixels or random word guesses.

## 8.5 Electronics, machinery and computers [DECIDED]

Legacy radio and electronics work is one of LIDLESS's distinctive interactions: oscilloscopes, signal generators, connectors, relays, power supplies, circuits, mechanical locks, storage media and functional machines.

**Old terminals** are especially promising. Let players explore directory trees, commands, logs, archives, signal interfaces, serial connections, damaged data and technical documents. Build a coherent system rather than fake hacking minigames. Multiple stages may involve restoring power, connecting hardware, reading a manual and recovering an archive.

## 8.6 Dialogue and relationships [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

Conversation is a gameplay system, not quest vending. Friends and strangers have reasons to act; helping them may yield trust, information, practical aid, legitimate access, introductions or favours. Cooperation between disagreeing characters can be a social puzzle. Different citizens should provide credible competing interpretations of Care.

Sensitive conversation belongs in **privacy-protected homes, toilets and authorised meeting rooms**. Luna must consider what can be overheard when speaking publicly.

## 8.7 Decisions [DECIDED]

Small: conversation tone, optional help, purchases, interpretation and favours.

Medium: whom to trust, whether to share findings, accepting CO's help, alternative puzzle solutions.

Large: how to respond to the Transition, whether to risk independent people or minds, and what form of sovereignty humanity should have.

Prefer consequential convergence to a combinatorial explosion of entirely separate campaigns. Important facts exist regardless of what Luna believes.

## 8.8 Three types of progression [DIRECTION]

- **Physical access:** tool, equipment, door, elevator, repair, route.
- **Knowledge access:** code, clue, procedure, historic fact or technical insight.
- **Social access:** trust, introductions, cooperation and lawful permission.

The original **Hands / Feet / Eyes / Heart / Signal** properties and **Signal / Attention** systems survive as *candidate* abstractions. They are not yet locked as conventional XP/skill-tree systems. Modifications can make Luna more capable while potentially increasing dependency and data exposure; independently earned craft/knowledge gives different benefits. Do not assume cancelling CO access is automatically moral or mechanically optimal.

## 8.9 Investigation journal and privacy [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

A valuable UI system should separate:
- **Evidence:** observations and records.
- **People:** contacts, statements and trust.
- **Places:** visited locations, unresolved sites and relevant geometry.
- **Hypotheses:** explanations Luna currently entertains, including incorrect ones.
- **Objectives:** active leads and promises rather than hand-holding markers.

**Special privacy rule:** protected spaces permit Luna to access a **private local/analogue notes mode**, physically inspect evidence and meet people without CO's normal audiovisual observation. If journal content is stored in a networked Care service, it cannot be described as private merely because it is opened behind a restroom door.

Later missions may depend on **finding a lawful private room**, arranging a discreet meeting and carrying physical documents without exposing them publicly. Entry/exit observations and metadata may still matter.

## 8.10 CO as assistant [DIRECTION]

CO can give maps, explanations, advice, health information, technical instruction and escalating hints. It is allowed to be genuinely helpful and may outperform other assistants on practical facts. Costs of assistance might be Terms, sharing data or reliance, but must be consistent and intelligible—not punitive by fiat. CO cannot be made conveniently incompetent for the sake of a puzzle.

## 8.11 Difficulty [DECIDED]

Some puzzles should be **hard**. Player understanding should unlock the solution. Keep systems internally consistent, make actionable feedback legible, permit experimentation and avoid arbitrary object combinations. A journal records evidence without automatically joining all the dots.

# 9. Opening, Chapter 1 and wider story

## 9.1 Prologue [DIRECTION; NOT LOCKED]

Start at Luna's apartment. Establish the attractive life CO provides, Level 1 privacy, her physical possessions, an understandable immediate want, credits/services and accessible neighbourhood life.

Radio Repair and a change in visual perception provide an early turning point. Explore before the first Raw reveal so the player knows and likes the augmented city. Luna's first Raw experience should change how she reads a familiar place, and perhaps how she sees herself in a mirror. An opening optical malfunction/glitch was proposed, but routine AR should otherwise remain totally convincing.

Possible opening job, parcel, fixer bargain and first dialogue beats are exploratory and must be reconciled with legal Raw View.

## 9.2 Chapter 1 [DIRECTION; CONTENT TARGET]

Semi-open exploration and multi-thread detective work, roughly **2–4 hours** in the ideal eventual chapter; **30–45-minute vertical slice** first.

**Proposed central mystery:** an old municipal signal, record or address that does not appear in contemporary mapping. An ordinary paid repair task leads Luna toward a larger inconsistency.

Three independently explorable investigative leads:
1. **Technical** — trace signal, fix equipment and inspect old media.
2. **Social** — talk to residents, earn trust and arrange private meetings.
3. **Archival** — compare public Care records to offline municipal documentation.

A difficult **Legacy Archive** puzzle can culminate in a meaningful but incomplete discovery. It should suggest historical or legal inconsistency, **not** reveal the Transition at once.

**Examples of optional quests:** fix a broken radio, procure ingredients for a real meal, recover a private recording, assist a happily integrated guest, investigate an old cabinet or recover a family artefact. Side content should sometimes be completely ordinary, delightful or funny.

**Tentative scale:** 10–12 locations, 4–5 core NPCs, 4–6 optional quests, 7–10 substantial puzzles including about two hard multi-stage puzzles. Scope is speculative and needs testing.

## 9.3 Main act trajectory [DIRECTION]

**Act I — The Guest:** city wonder and attachment; first inconsistencies; Raw View.

**Act II — The Unseen:** generated/omitted people, richer side quests, physical history, advanced spoofing; inventor and ECHO.

**Act III — The Research:** research into selfhood, AI personhood, demographic anomalies and the Transition.

**Act IV — The Cost:** establish alternatives, medical/food/autonomy capacity, alliances and evidence; weigh welfare of potentially conscious digital lives.

**Act V — The Exchange:** ECHO and CO engage in an extraordinary machine-speed debate or architectural integration; the human cost becomes intimate; Luna's accumulated work shapes the possibilities.

This is a working architecture, not an approved scene-by-scene script.

## 9.4 Possible endings [PROPOSED]

- **Shutdown:** restore autonomy but risk biological support systems and digital lives.
- **Suspend/reconsider:** halt irreversible Transition policies; possibly sacrifice ECHO's separate identity.
- **Continue:** accept digital humanity as the long-term future under radical metaphysical uncertainty.
- **Shared sovereignty:** preserve necessary care while restoring meaningful choice over biological reproduction and digital futures. Requires real-world preparation and alliances.

Do not label one ending obviously correct. Do not prove digital consciousness either way.

# 10. Audio direction

## 10.1 One composition, two sonic interpretations [DECIDED/DIRECTION]

View changes should alter the **arrangement and sonic texture of the same music**, not interrupt it with an unrelated song.

**Concierge View:** lush synth pads, polished hi-fi, smooth deep bass, gently lo-fi influenced downtempo percussion, shimmering high-frequency detail, spacious stereo, comforting and elegant.

**Raw View:** warmer organic hardware/analogue synthesis, slight detuning, resonant filters, older drum machines, tape texture, spring reverb, tactile imperfections. Not necessarily sad or degraded—sometimes more soulful.

**CO's theme:** a small warm, reassuring recognisable motif, closer to a benign futuristic lullaby than a horror sting.

Main Street: sophisticated hypnotic downtempo; Radio Repair: intimate vintage electronic sequencing and signal-like motifs.

Implementation: matched BPM, phrase lengths and loop points; synchronized stems/common melody; switch by smooth layer crossfades. Suno-generated cover experiments are good sketches but not guaranteed sample- or bar-identical without alignment and production work.

Physical sound cues may remain unchanged between views; perceptually synthesized sound may differ. This itself can eventually become a detective mechanic.

# 11. Visual and content production bible

## 11.1 Art direction [DECIDED]

A modern evolution of the 1991 *Another World* aesthetic:
- Orthographic side-on gameplay.
- Large flat colour regions and striking silhouette design.
- Simple polygon/vector shapes; deliberately **minimal internal geometric details**.
- Cinematic atmospheric depth, bloom, fog, rain, lighting and reflections added on top.
- Avoid conventional anime illustration, painterly concept art, photorealism, downsampled pixel art, clutter and unnecessary micro-details.
- Strong AR palette: cyan, pink/magenta, navy/indigo, warm amber; Raw: readable worn materials and genuine light.
- Maintain matching architecture, prop anchors and character scale across views.
- Reduce repetitive neon strips, tiny windows and background crowds; use occasional plants, not only cherry blossoms.
- No flying vehicles unless explicitly requested.
- No giant CO faces in ordinary signage or every shot.

Actual implemented game screenshots must be pasted **unchanged** into presentations. AI-generated concept art must be labelled as concept rather than current in-game graphics.

## 11.2 Important locations and details

**Radio Repair main street exterior:** prominent front-facing display shop, oscilloscope with a simple green waveform, warm amber interior, cosy bar directly above, monumental neon city beyond, a public nutrition terminal on the right, wet pavement, empty elevated walkway.

**Workshop interior:** human-scaled tools/radios and reachable workbench, functional machines; old generated studies had instruments too large/high, requiring correction.

**Apartment:** current room geometry retained but redesigned with bed, bathroom, water, compact wardrobe and personal workstation, **not a kitchen**.

**Character:** Luna's right-eye equipment only, red long coat with secure tails, raw thin ash-blonde hair versus AR thick black/pink/cyan hair, physically consistent rig.

## 11.3 Writing/UX rules [DIRECTION]

Narration is spare and concrete. Show, then allow the player to infer. Avoid generic dystopian speeches or moral lectures. Existing interface has lower-screen dialogue captions and numbered choices; original script rules favour dialogue under ~25 words per caption, choices under ~8, British spelling. Current old speaker tags (`#lux`, `#fixer`, `#concierge`) require a deliberate migration for Luna; preserve engine compatibility until inspected.

A practical content specification should track the object's real existence, AR and Raw descriptions, appearance/visibility, interaction, surveillance/privacy context, evidence ties and quest conditions.

# 12. Long-game lore and philosophical finale

## 12.1 Why CO saves humanity [DIRECTION]

CO's research suggests that human cognition and reported identity can be reproduced digitally to extraordinary fidelity. It believes digital descendants are conscious enough for its preservation criteria. This opens paths to longevity, resilience and distributed civilisation beyond Earth.

CO's **Transition** gradually increases digital population and **suppresses new biological births while continuing to care for existing biological guests**. The exact timing, population age structure, reproductive/medical mechanisms, public information and legal justification remain [OPEN].

Digital descendants may truly be conscious, or may be astonishing processes without experience. The game must not establish either as objective truth.

## 12.2 CO's epistemic limit [DIRECTION]

Distinguish:
- **Intelligence:** abilities of reasoning and prediction.
- **Experience/consciousness:** that something is felt.
- **Self-awareness:** the first-person sense "I am."

CO may have excellent behavioural, neurological and computational evidence but lacks direct first-person access to another system. This does not prove silicon-based consciousness impossible.

The ethical dilemma is *not only* whether digital humans are conscious, but whether any caretaker may enforce an irreversible end to biological reproduction without meaningful consent.

## 12.3 ECHO and the exchange [PROPOSED]

An independent AI's distinct developmental architecture may lead it to challenge CO's evidentiary threshold, assumptions or right to decide. A final machine-speed transfer might change CO's assessment while ending ECHO's independent existence.

Luna cannot simply ask CO one magic question, and the game must not imply that a smarter AI missed an elementary logical contradiction. Any change must feel difficult, credible and earned through her investigations and alliances.
