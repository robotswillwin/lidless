# LIDLESS — The director's decisions since the bible v0.4
**22–23 October 2026 · a record of what the director has decided since the bible v0.4 (21 October), in conversation**

The bible is unchanged: the director updates it. Until then, where this record and the bible differ, **this record
holds**. Each decision is the director's, in plain words; what is still open says so.

## The world

1. **Raw view is legal.** The Concierge knows when someone uses it, and does not recommend it. People still give it
   their data everywhere except in privacy zones, so it is not illegal. How Luna comes by raw view is **open**: a new
   job that needs it, buying it, or being given it. In the game today the fixer switches it on; the director has
   suggested the old laptop might be where she learns of it.
2. **No blind spots, only legal privacy zones**: homes, public restrooms, approved meeting rooms. Nowhere else is
   hidden from the Concierge. A long stay in a public restroom, or going there too often, is noticed: not illegal, but
   the Concierge says so.
3. **Two books.**
   - **Her journal**, on her device: objectives, people, places (the map) and evidence. The Concierge can read it.
   - **Her notebook**, on paper: secrets, in her own hand, read and written only in a privacy zone.
   The map, the Concierge's interface and the verbs look neutral and clean. The notebook is analogue: paper and pen.
4. **Feeding is at the wrist.**
   - Main nutrition and medicine come from the city's nutrition and health stations. There are no pharmacies.
   - A station is a central column with the Concierge's C and two chairs built into it, back to back, the port for
     the right wrist in the armrest. In the Concierge's view it is white and calm, with cyan light; in raw view, worn
     machinery: pipes, gauges, pumps, glowing cartridges.
   - In use: she sits, rests her wrist, a scanning light reads her, and it is done. It is not a goal of the game to
     manage her hunger: she uses a station now and then, and otherwise says she doesn't need it right now.
   - Different people sit at the main street's station over time.
5. **Luna wears a device on each wrist**, in the style of her eyepiece.
6. **Food is legal but very expensive**, and hardly needed. Bars are fine: people, the richer above all, still drink
   and smoke, and their nutrition repairs the damage. Restaurants are on top floors, for the rich or rare occasions.
   Shops do not mainly sell food.
7. **Bars and shops are real places**, open in raw view too: the Concierge's view only dresses them up.
8. **Nobody sleeps outside.** The homeless man is gone from the game. The Concierge hides very few people from its
   view: only to protect others, from an accident or a crime in progress, and that is rare.
9. **Helper robots** keep the city clean, keep it safe, and carry things from place to place. They are small and
   rare, not everywhere at once; more of them come in later in the game. Drones pass now and then.
   - The **civic guardian** patrols the main street. Talked to, it speaks of keeping society safe, and asks her to
     tell it if anything odd is happening.
   - The **maintenance robot** welds the backstreet's panel.
10. **The city.**
    - The flying car and the train stay.
    - The Concierge's calm face on its billboard is fine where it is, but not to be used more.
    - Credits are digital.
11. **The main street**, left to right: the radio repair with a bar above it; the nutrition station; the cocktail bar
    with a restaurant on the floor above; the corner shop; the public restroom at the far right. The restroom is a
    privacy zone for private meetings and her notes.
12. **The old computer** is a **retro laptop** that she comes by easily. It may be where she learns about raw view, or
    something like it. **Open**: how it reaches her and what is on it. It works offline, like an old Unix terminal,
    with files to read for clues.

## Luna and what is shown

13. **Undressed** (after the shower, at home) stays, for now. Decency matters, but so does rawness: the outfit is
    censored, a mosaic over nipples and vulva only. No other nudity, and no explicit sex.
14. **Her things** are painted objects in her inventory, as now.
15. **Only the Concierge calls Luna by her name**, in her eye: its card, its messages, its answers. Every other bot
    and system (the nutrition stations, the civic guardian, the city's machines) calls her "guest", for anonymity.
    "Guest" is also how the Concierge speaks of anyone it does not name: "She is a guest, dining", "All guests
    welcome". (The bible has it address its guests as "guest"; this holds instead.)
16. **Her readouts** in the Concierge's card: her heart (it climbs as she hurries, and as the Concierge's attention
    grows), her credits, her nutrition. Nutrition is never something to plan for or keep track of: good, or
    optimal after a station, nothing else.
17. **A deeply integrated guest** on the main street, much more integrated with the Concierge than Luna: more
    devices, goggles over both eyes, clothes made of light, from whom she learns about the levels. In the game:
    **Felix**, a Level 4 guest and happy for it, inside the cocktail bar (his name and his words are proposals).
18. **The connection levels**, from the director's concept sheet
    (`docs/art/LIDLESS_Connection_Levels_Concept_2026-10-22.webp`): the same person at each level, in both views.
    Every piece of equipment has the same shape, size and place in both views; only its look changes, idealised,
    clean and calm in the Concierge's, real materials, wear and technology in raw view. The more connected someone
    is, the more of it shows. Less connection is more personal agency; more integration, more mediation.
    - **0 Unregistered.** Disconnected from Care, no access to services; visible, and marked in the Concierge's view
      (a red "Unregistered"). Biological, no implants: a patch over the lidless right eye (19). (The bible: earlier
      lore counts unregistered humans as weather.)
    - **1 Basic Care** (Luna). Minimum legal integration; access to services. A simple eyepiece over the right eye,
      a wrist interface.
    - **2 Enhanced Care.** More comfort, more features. Dual optics over the right eye, subtle fluid lines, a wrist
      interface.
    - **3 Integrated Care.** Clear integration. A headband module, neck integration, visible fluid lines.
    - **4 Deep Integration.** Mostly mediated. Dual glasses, neck and spine integration, prominent fluid lines.
    - **5 Full Integration.** Almost entirely mediated. Advanced dual glasses, integrated fluid systems, a small
      integrated back unit, robotic hands and legs.

    Up to Deep Integration there is no external hardware, and nobody wears a helmet. Where the sheet has an eyepiece
    over the left eye, or connectors anywhere but the wrists, that is the image model's mistake (19, 20).
19. **Lidless.** Nobody here has two eyelids: they are removed at birth. The eye without them is the right one, and
    every eyepiece is over it, always; a device over the left eye in a picture is the image model's mistake. The
    unregistered, with no eyepiece, wear a patch over it. The inventor, met later in the game, may be the exception.
20. **Connectors are at the wrists only**, at every level. What a higher level adds elsewhere is implants and fluid
    lines, never a port.
21. **No people painted into a background**, not even behind a window or far off: every person is a character of
    their own, drawn at true scale against Luna. And everything keeps the flat vector style: simple shapes, few
    tones, no fine detail.
22. **The cocktail bar can be entered.** Felix is inside, and Luna is shocked by the prices of real drinks: she
    cannot afford even a beer. A beer 450 credits, a gin 680, a twelve-year whisky 1,400, against her allowance
    (312). The bar's machine pours; Felix, once met, pays.
23. **Where a cutaway shows a basement, the ground beside it is cut away too**: under the street outside, gravel,
    earth, pipes and cables, not a street running on below the floor.
24. **The outside of a place matches its inside**: what the street shows through a window is the room she walks
    into.
25. **Most signs are the Concierge's**: shop names, neon, sign boards and screens are projections, not there at all in
    raw view, where the wall or the band they hang on is bare. Raw view keeps only physical remains: an old torn
    poster, a word faded on brick. **No Concierge symbols in raw view** either: its C on the stations, the guardian,
    the drones and the robots, and its closed eye on the privacy rooms, are its projections; in raw view the metal
    and the walls are bare.
26. **Luna's tower has rooms inside**: Entrance 3 from the backstreet leads to the lift hall; the lift goes to the
    floors (9 and 14 so far), each a corridor of flat doors; her flat is off the corridor of floor 14. The lift is a
    machine of the city and calls her "guest".

27. **Ways to the next place are always marked; a door only once she knows it**: a marker at the end of a street
    always; a door's marker once she has looked at it or tried it, so not every way in shows at once.
28. **A building is as wide on the street as the rooms inside it.** Its windows show the part of its rooms behind
    them; the rest of its front is wall without windows. The radio repair and the cocktail bar are 8 m wide, their
    street doors at the left as inside. Every room is at true scale, upper storeys too: the restaurant's tables are
    0.75 m high, as the bar's below.
29. **Time of day.** The Concierge's view has no day: it is always evening or night. Raw view shows the real time:
    by day, the city's smog and fog, as the raw pictures are now; at night perhaps a darker raw view, later. So a
    morning is possible: it looks like evening in the Concierge's view and like smog by day in raw view.
30. **The privacy sign.** Every privacy location shows the closed eye, in the Concierge's interface. When privacy is
    broken, by a call or an emergency, it changes to the open eye, and back when it ends. Luna can call the Concierge
    from a private home, never from a public privacy room (a public restroom, an approved meeting room).
31. **The photograph in Luna's flat** is a projection: in raw view its frame is empty.
32. **Athena**, Luna's older neighbour and mentor (the opening, "The Invitation"), was downgraded from a higher level
    to Level 1, and has now probably gone to Level 0, which Luna does not know yet.
33. **Luna waking** is a close-up, as the feeding at the station is.

## Production

34. **Music** is the director's: one composition in a digital and an analogue arrangement, blended as the view

35. **English only**, for now.

## Luna on screen

36. **Luna turns.** Seen from the side as she walks, she turns her back to us as she goes in at a door (every door is
    drawn in a wall we look at; only the ends of a street are walked off side on), and to us as she comes out of one,
    after any change of clothes (the shower ends on her undressed and censored, facing us), when she has stood still a
    while, and when the player taps her; each until she moves. Every outfit has a front, those she goes out in a back.
37. **She blinks**, her left eye only (her right has no lids, 19), side on and facing us, now and then.
38. **Athena's flat (9-07) is a whole home on one floor**, as Luna's is: a shower and a basin, a kitchen corner, her
    bed, her chair, her workbench. (Two storeys were considered: the tower's other floors are drawn smaller than the
    one in view, so an upper floor would not be at Luna's scale.)
39. **Talking to the Concierge**, its card shows only the latest exchange: her answer and what it says to it.
40. **In the opening Luna covers for Athena**: asked by the Concierge whether Athena was home, the one thing she can
    say is what Athena asked her to, "Yes. Her music was loud." The story does not branch here. Before that, she does
    not leave her flat until she has read her messages, and at 9-07 she rings the bell before she asks the door to let
    her in. A paper read close up stays until she puts it down.
41. **The tower's other floors** are drawn at the same scale as the one she is on, aligned with it (the lift over the
    lift), each lived in its own way, and dimmed, as the neighbours' flats around her home are. On floor nine nothing
    stands open: flat 9-06's door is shut, and the delivery box and the cleaning robot, which had no part to play, are
    gone.
42. **Close-ups to explore.** A thing worth a closer look (a desk, a workbench) is a close-up of its own: tapped, Luna
    goes up to it and the view goes in close, where its things can be looked at, used, opened and taken one by one;
    Step back returns her to where she stood. Her own desk and Athena's workbench are the first; each has a drawer.
43. **Nothing is locked for the sake of it.** A drawer, a cupboard, a box opens, and may hold nothing of value; or Luna
    says there is nothing there for her, or no reason to (a neighbour's door: "No reason to knock"); or it is not a
    thing she can do anything with at all. No locked drawers, nothing that will not open.
44. **What moves is drawn live**, in code, not painted, so it is alive: an oscilloscope's line creeps and breathes, a
    clock's hands keep the time. Athena's oscilloscope is the first: Luna can turn its two knobs, one for the line's
    shape and one for its speed, a bonus that nothing waits on. The fixer's monitor and his clock are live too, and
    so are the tower's lift displays (one lift for the whole tower, called up and down by the neighbours), a camera
    in the backstreet that turns to follow her, raw view's alone, and the restroom's hand dryer.
45. **Luna has no terminal, no radio and no valves.** Everything of hers is the Concierge's voice and gestures; only old
    relics (Athena's, the fixer's) have keys and dials. In raw view her desk is nearly bare. The first build's radio,
    its valve and the voice under the static are cut: there is no mysterious voice. On her desk now: a Newton's
    cradle, real in both views, that she can set swinging; a hologram puzzle of three rings, the Concierge's alone; and
    her photo frame, showing the people in her life in turn: her, her and Athena (Athena's first pictures), a party.
46. **A tap looks.** Tapping a thing looks at it; it never picks a thing up or uses it: taking and using are chosen at
    the ball. A tap still goes through a door or up close to a desk, and talks with someone she can talk with.
