# LIDLESS — Luna's flat, redesigned: the plan
**21 October 2026 · for the director's review before any art is made**

## The brief, in short

The flat still reads as a small apartment from our time: a fridge, a kettle, cups and a kitchen make no sense if
people no longer eat. It should say something about the Concierge's society and about Luna.

- **A hotel room someone has made her own.** The Concierge provides a comfortable, standardised, efficient baseline:
  good light, clean surfaces, climate, sleep, a beautiful augmented view. Level 1 accommodation is modest but not
  miserable, and welcoming in its view. Luna owns a handful of real, slightly unusual things.
- **Raw view is not a ruin.** The Concierge still provides safe, working accommodation. What raw view shows is that the
  beautiful surfaces are augmented: bare industrial panels, standard equipment, a grim city outside, harsher light.
  Her own things stay as they are, because they are real. That is the line between what the Concierge provides and
  what Luna chose.
- **One living plant.** Lush in the Concierge's view; in raw view small and struggling, but alive, because she has
  kept it alive. A quiet metaphor for the whole game.
- **A place of her own.** One of the few places the Concierge is not watching or listening: somewhere she can think,
  investigate, bring trusted people and say what she cannot say outside. It should become one of the game's most
  important recurring places, not only where it begins.
- Keep the composition, the warm colours and the large central window.

## Decided with the director: water and nutrition

- **Water** is collected centrally or delivered. Every home and every public place has it.
- **Nutrition** is taken at stations: you sit in a chair, the station measures all your vitals and injects a cocktail
  made for you through a port in your lower back. Nobody eats; there is no food and no cooking.

So the flat has a water point and no nutrition at all. The main street's dining machine can become a public nutrition
station: the generated woman at it sits in its chair (see Open questions).

## Light and life

The room's light stays warm in the Concierge's view and turns harsher, whiter in raw view, as the engine already does.
The dormant screen keeps a faint standby glow. The terminal's screen glows when she is near it. Everything outside stays
as it is now: the city in layers, the smoke, the lights, the flying car.

## Open questions for the director

1. **Privacy at home.** The world bible says the Concierge sees everything she sees, even at home: her visor feeds her
   eye to it all the time. The brief makes home one of the few places it does not watch. My proposal: a Courtesy, a
   promise it keeps. Homes are private unless she summons it, or her vital signs call it in. The feed still flows; it
   chooses not to look. Then what counts as home, and as summoning, becomes a wording puzzle, as the bible's other
   Courtesies do.
2. **Her terminal.** All technology belongs to the Concierge except what people make by hand. Is her terminal one of
   its eyes, a standard device she has dressed with an old keyboard? Or a relic she keeps working, and so private?
   The first fits the world; the second gives her a tool.
3. **"Dinner is served."** The Concierge's catchphrase, and the street's dining machine. With nutrition at stations,
   "dinner" can be its hotel manners for the chair and the injection. Keep the line?
4. **Her port.** Her lower-back port shows in her underwear outfit. Should it be drawn: plain in raw view and smoothed
   away in the Concierge's?
